01
Week 1–3 · Future Health

Future Health
Self-Care System

A speculative design exploration focusing on the interface between body, material, and technology.

Part 01

Knowledge Collection

A crucial organ system in the human body is the gastrointestinal system. However, whether in terms of the complexity of medical examinations or daily observation, it poses significant challenges. It is also the only area that requires internal examination during basic diagnostic procedures.

Research reference material
Part 02

Proposal & Experiment

Current Pain Points:

1. The intestinal tract itself hosts a vast and diverse population of microorganisms, and the organ structure is deep and winding.

2. Medical examinations are primitive and complex.

3. Diet planning is troublesome and lacks intelligent guidance.

Here are the nodes for my transparent material. I found that adjusting the material in the standard panel didn't achieve the desired effect, so I used the node editor.

This showcases my node setup for achieving a lens-like material.

This interactive simulation demonstrates two core states of the lens system:

Left — Dynamic Imaging  Simulates the process of the device capturing and displaying real-time internal structure imagery while in motion.

Right — Depth Adjustment  Illustrates how users can externally interact to control the focal depth, enabling visual exploration from the surface to the interior.

Part 03

My Outcome

My design direction envisions a possible future scenario where people can check basic intestinal conditions at home using an observation scope, with the device being both portable and stationary.

It is equipped with manual tools to assist with movement and angle adjustment, and may even include matching panels that can be installed on the visual interface of refrigerators, working together with diet planning to provide assistance and recommendations.

Future Technologies: Home-Based Health Care (Lowering the threshold for medical access), Advanced Penetrative/Lens Technology.

Week 4–6 · Spatial Intelligence

空间智能

A speculative design exploration focusing on spatial interaction and data-driven environments.

Part 01

Knowledge Collection

The question I want to explore is: In 2036, when we sit at our desks to work or study, how will we develop our ideas? From what I've read, the history of human thought can be seen in three major shifts: Third, external symbols—writing, books, notes—tools that freed memory from the brain's physical limits. So, what would a fourth evolution look like? I haven't found the right words for it yet. let's call it "3D Thinking.” It's more like this: if we think of our current way of thinking—searching on screens, pondering on paper—as "two-dimensional," then the next evolution must come with some kind of fundamental shift in our framework or method.
